Who Is Sayed Asif?
QA Engineer & Software Testing

A practical approach to software testing, quality assurance, defect detection, and reliable product delivery.

Published March 30, 2026 by Sayed Asif

I'm Sayed Asif, a QA Engineer focused on software testing and quality assurance. My approach to testing is centred on understanding how a product is expected to work, validating that behaviour, and looking for scenarios where the software may not behave as intended.

Software quality is more than checking whether a feature works on the happy path. Effective testing also considers invalid inputs, edge cases, unexpected user behaviour, integration points, and the impact that a defect can have on the overall user experience.

You can learn more about my background, testing approach, and areas of expertise on my About page .

Core Testing Competencies

My testing work covers several areas of software quality assurance, with an emphasis on structured testing, clear defect reporting, and repeatable validation.

  • Manual & Exploratory Testing: Testing functional workflows, business logic, user scenarios, edge cases, and unexpected behaviour.
  • API Testing: Using tools such as Postman to validate API requests, responses, status codes, data handling, and error scenarios.
  • Defect Tracking & Management: Using Jira to document, track, and communicate defects with clear reproduction steps and supporting details.
  • Test Automation: Building and maintaining automated regression tests using Selenium with JavaScript where automation can provide repeatable coverage and reduce repetitive manual work.

How I Approach Software Testing

I start by understanding the requirements and expected behaviour of a feature. From there, I consider both positive and negative scenarios and identify areas where the application could behave differently from what is expected.

When I find a defect, I focus on making the issue easy to understand and reproduce. A useful defect report should provide clear steps, expected behaviour, actual behaviour, and relevant evidence so that the development team can investigate the problem efficiently.

I also consider regression impact. A change that fixes one area of an application can sometimes affect another, which is why repeatable regression coverage is an important part of a practical QA process.

The Role of QA in Software Development

I believe QA works best as a collaborative part of the software development lifecycle rather than as a final checkpoint before release.

Testing can provide valuable feedback throughout development by identifying unexpected behaviour, clarifying requirements, and highlighting areas that may need additional validation.

My goal is to provide practical testing feedback that helps teams understand product risks and make informed decisions about software quality.
